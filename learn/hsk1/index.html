How to use this book

Do one day at a time, about 25 minutes a day. Each lesson has only 5 new words and 1 new idea, so you never have to learn too much at once. The book runs for 6 weeks, matching your 6-week HSK1 study plan: Monday to Saturday are lesson days, Sunday is a review day, and Week 6 is for practice tests.

Your daily routine:

  1. Warm up (3 min). Read yesterday's 5 words out loud.
  2. Learn the words (7 min). Read each new word 5 times: say the pinyin, say the meaning, look at the character.
  3. Learn the idea (5 min). Read the "How it works" part and the example sentences out loud.
  4. Practice (7 min). Answer the practice questions on paper, then check the answers below them.
  5. Finish (3 min). Write each new character 3 times and tick the day off.

Three simple rules make this work:

  • Say everything out loud. Chinese is a spoken language first. Reading silently does not train your tones.
  • Don't skip Sunday. Review days are where words move into long-term memory.
  • Missed a day? Just continue from where you stopped. Do not try to do two days at once.

You do not need to write characters perfectly for HSK1. The test is listening and reading, so recognising a character is enough.

Before Day 1: the three building blocks

Every Chinese word has three parts: a character (what you see), pinyin (how to say it, in Latin letters) and a tone (the shape of your voice). Learn these three ideas once, and every lesson after this gets easier.

1. Pinyin = Chinese written in our alphabet

Pinyin tells you how to pronounce a character. is written nǐ hǎo. Most letters sound close to English, but a few are different:

Pinyin Sounds like Example
x "sh" with a big smile xièxie (thank you)
q "ch" with a big smile qù (go)
j "j" with a big smile jǐ (how many)
zh "j" in "jump" Zhōngguó (China)
c "ts" in "cats" cài (dish of food)
z "ds" in "beds" zàijiàn (goodbye)
r between "r" and the "s" in "pleasure" rén (person)
ü say "ee" with round lips nǚ (woman)

2. The four tones

The same sound with a different tone is a different word. mā (mother) and mǎ (horse) are not the same word! The mark above the vowel shows the tone.

Tone Mark Your voice Example
1st ā High and flat, like singing one long note mā (mum)
2nd á Goes up, like asking "What?" lái (come)
3rd ǎ Dips down low, then up a little wǒ (I)
4th à Drops sharply, like saying "No!" shì (to be)
Neutral a Short and light, no mark ma (question word)

Two tone rules you will hear all the time:

  • Two 3rd tones in a row: the first one becomes a 2nd tone. nǐ hǎo is said ní hǎo.
  • bù (not) becomes bú before a 4th tone. is said bú shì.

3. Characters are built from pieces

You don't need to write characters perfectly for HSK1, only recognise them. It helps to notice the pieces. (good) is (woman) + (child). (you) has on the left, which means "person". , , and all have (mouth) on the left, because they are about the mouth.

Grammar in one sentence

Chinese grammar is friendly: verbs never change (no "I am / he is / they were"), there are no plurals, and the basic word order is the same as English: Who + does + what. = I drink tea.
